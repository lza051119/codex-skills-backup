# Professional Hairstyle Consultation Framework

## Evidence Checklist

Use photos and user answers to classify:

- Face outline: oval, round, square, rectangle, heart, diamond, triangle, mixed.
- Vertical proportion: short, balanced, or long face; forehead share; lower-third strength.
- Horizontal proportion: temple width, cheekbone width, jaw width, visual center of mass.
- Head shape: flat crown, high crown, flat occipital bone, full occipital bone, wide parietal area.
- Hairline: straight, widow's peak, M-shaped recession, high temples, low forehead, uneven corners.
- Density: sparse, medium, dense; note scalp visibility at front/crown.
- Strand and behavior: fine, medium, coarse; soft, stiff, oily, dry, frizzy, wavy, curly.
- Growth pattern: cowlick, crown swirl, side hair sticking out, forward growth, backward growth.
- Lifestyle: daily styling time, wash frequency, product comfort, salon frequency, formal constraints.

## Design Rules

- Add height when the face is round, jaw is broad, or the top looks flat.
- Avoid excessive crown height when the face is already long.
- Keep side volume controlled when the head is wide at the temples or parietal area.
- Avoid high fades on long faces unless the top stays low and balanced.
- Use fringe to shorten a long forehead; use open forehead or side part to lengthen a short face.
- Use soft texture to reduce a strong jaw; use cleaner edges to sharpen a soft jaw.
- Preserve density at the hairline when the front is sparse; do not over-texturize thinning areas.
- Use low taper or low fade for conservative, natural grow-out.
- Use mid fade, crop, or textured layers for stronger change and a cleaner outline.
- Use perm only when the user accepts maintenance and the cut needs volume or movement the natural hair cannot provide.
- Use color only when it supports skin tone, maintenance budget, and workplace constraints.

## Common Plan Types

- Low taper textured crop: clean sides, controlled width, easy daily styling.
- Scissor cut layered medium length: softer, professional, better for uneven head shape.
- Side-part contour: mature, business-friendly, good for balanced or slightly round faces.
- Curtain or comma fringe: useful for higher forehead and softer style goals.
- French crop / Caesar variation: good for receding corners or lower-maintenance texture.
- Two-block variation: works when hair is dense enough and the user accepts styling.
- Wolf cut / shag variation: only when length, texture, and style tolerance support it.
- Buzz / crew variation: only when head shape, hairline, and confidence fit; can expose asymmetry.

## Red Flags

- User wants a style that requires daily heat styling but says they will not style hair.
- User has stiff side hair but wants soft long sides without perm or product.
- User has a long face but wants very high top volume and shaved sides.
- User has sparse front hair but wants heavy thinning or separated wet-look fringe.
- User wants major bleaching with damaged hair or low maintenance.

When there is a red flag, explain the conflict and propose a workable nearby version.
